Security tips for travel

November 2, 2026

Travel is when your usual safety nets are furthest away: a different phone plan, unfamiliar Wi-Fi, and a higher chance of losing a device. A few minutes of prep before you leave makes a big difference.

Before you go

  • Make sure your codes work without a phone signal. Text message codes may not arrive abroad or on a local SIM. Authenticator app codes are made on your device and work offline, which the FTC notes is also safer. See App codes vs text messages.
  • Back up your authenticator and leave a copy at home or in encrypted cloud storage. See Back up your authenticator.
  • Pack your recovery codes separately from your phone, for example printed in your carry-on or saved in a password manager.
  • Update your devices and turn on find-and-erase, so a lost phone can be locked from anywhere.

On the road

  • Be careful on public Wi-Fi. The FTC says most public Wi-Fi is usually safe now because most sites encrypt traffic, but a fake website can be encrypted too. Check the address before you sign in. CISA suggests switching to mobile data for banking and shopping.
  • Skip public USB charging stations. CISA advises against charging from public USB ports or shared computers. Bring your own charger and plug it into a wall outlet.
  • Turn off Bluetooth when you’re not using it, as CISA also suggests.
  • Don’t sign in on hotel or shared computers. You can’t know what’s running on them.

If your phone goes missing abroad

Follow our lost phone checklist. Your recovery codes and backup are what get you back in from a borrowed device or a new phone.

Sources

  2. Are Public Wi-Fi Networks Safe? What You Need To Know (FTC)
  3. Use Two-Factor Authentication To Protect Your Accounts (FTC)
