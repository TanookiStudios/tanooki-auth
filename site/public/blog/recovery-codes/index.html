Recovery codes: the spare key you’ll be glad you kept

November 2, 2026

When you turn on two-factor login, most websites show you a list of recovery codes (sometimes called backup codes). It’s tempting to click past them. Don’t.

What they are

Recovery codes are one-time passwords for emergencies. If you lose your phone or your authenticator, one of these codes gets you in instead. Google gives you a set of ten, each usable once, and making a new set cancels the old ones. GitHub works the same way.

Where to keep them

  • Somewhere that isn’t your phone. The whole point is surviving a lost phone.
  • Printed and stored safely, such as with your important papers, or
  • In a password manager. GitHub suggests this.

Tanooki Auth also has a Recovery codes spot on each account, which is handy as long as your Tanooki backup lives somewhere other than the phone too.

What not to do

  • Never share them. Google is clear that it will only ask for one when you’re signing in, never by phone, email or text. Anyone else asking is a scammer.
  • Don’t keep only a screenshot in your phone’s photo library.
  • After you use one, cross it off. When you’re running low, make a fresh set.

Set up more than one way in

GitHub also recommends adding more than one two-factor method, such as an app and a security key, so losing one doesn’t lock you out. That’s good advice everywhere.
