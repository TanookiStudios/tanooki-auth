What to do if you lose your phone

November 2, 2026

Losing your phone feels awful, especially when it holds your two-factor codes. Take a breath. Work through this list in order.

1. Lock it and find it

  • iPhone: Apple says to sign in at iCloud.com/find and turn on Lost Mode. Don’t remove the phone from your account, so Activation Lock keeps it useless to a thief.
  • Android: Google says to use google.com/android/find to ring it, lock it and show a message on the screen, or erase it if you need to.

2. Call your carrier

Ask them to suspend service on the phone, so nobody can receive your texts or calls. Apple also suggests reporting a theft to the police. Google notes your carrier can block the phone by its IMEI number, so it’s worth writing that number down today, while you still have the phone.

3. Change your most important passwords

Start with your Apple or Google account and your email, then your bank. Sign out of sessions you don’t recognize.

4. Get back into accounts that use codes

This is where preparation pays off:

  • If you saved recovery codes, each one gets you in once. Google explains how backup codes work.
  • If you have a backup of your authenticator, restore it on your new phone. In Tanooki Auth that’s your encrypted backup file and its password, or Premium sync from another device.
  • If you have neither, you’ll need each website’s account recovery process. It can be slow, on purpose.

Before it happens

The FTC recommends turning on your phone’s find-and-erase feature, using a passcode of at least six digits and backing up regularly. Add two things for your codes: save recovery codes, and keep a backup of your authenticator somewhere other than the phone. See Back up your authenticator.
