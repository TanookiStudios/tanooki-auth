Back up your authenticator before you need to

November 2, 2026

An authenticator app holds the secrets that make your codes. If the only copy is on one phone and that phone is lost, broken or wiped, every account that uses it needs a recovery. A backup turns that disaster into a ten-minute chore.

What a good backup looks like

CISA’s general advice on backups applies here: back up often so losing a device doesn’t mean losing data, keep a copy somewhere separate, encrypt your backups, and keep the password somewhere safe.

For an authenticator, that means:

  • Encrypted. Your backup holds the keys to your accounts. It should be locked with a password only you know.
  • Somewhere else. Not just on the same phone. A computer, a USB stick or a cloud drive are all fine for an encrypted file.
  • Tested. Restore it once, so you know it works and you remember the password.

How the big apps do it

Different apps take different approaches. Google Authenticator can sync your codes to your Google Account. Microsoft Authenticator backs up to iCloud on iPhone or to a Microsoft account on Android, and Microsoft notes it only restores to the same kind of phone. Our comparison pages lay out how each app handles backups, with sources.
