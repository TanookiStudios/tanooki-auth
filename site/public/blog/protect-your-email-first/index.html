Protect your email first

November 2, 2026

If you only have time to secure one account today, make it your email. Here’s why.

Email is the master key

Almost every website has a “Forgot password?” link, and almost every one sends the reset to your email. The FTC warns that a hacker who controls your email can reset the passwords on your other accounts and lock you out of them. CISA’s Turn On MFA page tells the story of a scammer who took over one email account and used it to get into the bank and shopping accounts linked to it.

Lock it down

  • Use a strong password you don’t use anywhere else. A password manager makes this easy.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication, ideally a passkey or an authenticator app rather than text messages. Our guides cover Gmail (Google), Yahoo and Proton.
  • Check your recovery email and phone number. Make sure they’re yours and current.
  • Save your recovery codes somewhere off your phone.
  • Review signed-in devices and sign out of any you don’t recognize.

Signs your email was hacked

The FTC lists the warning signs: you can’t log in, you get a notice about a password change or a sign-in from a device you don’t know, or friends get messages from you that you didn’t send. After a hack, also check your settings for forwarding rules, which can quietly send copies of your mail to someone else.

If it happens, the FTC recommends changing the password, signing out of all devices, turning on two-factor, checking the recovery details, deleting forwarding rules you didn’t set up, and warning your contacts.

Then do the same for your other important accounts. CISA suggests social media, shopping and banking next.
