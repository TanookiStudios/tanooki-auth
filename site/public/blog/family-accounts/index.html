Helping your family stay safe online

November 2, 2026

Most families have one person everyone calls when something on the computer goes wrong. If that’s you, a little setup now saves a lot of panic later.

Talk about scams, kindly

The FTC’s Pass It On campaign is built for exactly this: short, friendly material about common scams, like fake tech support and impersonation, to share with people you care about. The message that matters most: no real company will ever call or text asking for a code or password. If someone does, hang up and call the company back on a number you already know.

Turn on two-factor together

Sit down with them and turn on two-factor for their email first, then their bank. CISA notes that any MFA is far better than none, so pick the option they’ll actually use. An authenticator app is a good fit for most people. Our guides make it step by step.

Plan for the lost phone

  • Help them save recovery codes and put a printed copy with their important papers.
  • Set up an encrypted backup of their authenticator, and make sure they know where the password is.
  • Write down who to call and what to do if their phone is lost. Our lost phone checklist is a good start.

Kids’ accounts

If you manage a child’s Google Account with Family Link, Google notes that resetting their password turns off their 2-Step Verification. You both get an email, and they can turn it back on. Remember to do that.

Sharing an account

Some accounts really are shared, like a streaming service or a utility. Don’t share your whole authenticator to cover it. Tanooki Auth Premium can share one account with someone you trust, and the Family plan covers up to five people. Whatever you use, share just the account, not everything.
